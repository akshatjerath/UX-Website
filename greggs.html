How does a cheap bakery stay cheap for 25 more years?

The strategy

Keep the sausage roll cheap. Let other things pay for it.

Affordability is built into how the business makes money, so it can't be quietly withdrawn like a discount.

Ten courses, from 1939 to 2050.

Same market, opposite bets

Greggs · NorthNewcastle, 1939Affordable, national, takeaway-led. Built for breadth.
Pret · SouthHampstead, 1984Premium and London-heavy: 273 of its 434 shops were in London (Dec 2022).

A Sheffield Hallam study sorted English towns into "Greggs" or "Pret" from shop locations alone, and the line it drew matched household income.

It's the Costco hot-dog trick: the hero price is protected by structure, so it can't be quietly withdrawn like a discount.

A world where good food is never a luxury

To keep great food within everyone's reach, fresh, fast and fairly priced, wherever and however people live in 2050.

Always Fresh. Always Tasty.Fresh. Fair. Forever.

Same rhythm as today's line. It adds fairness (health and price) and permanence.

Warm functional futurism

Blue#00558FYellow#EAB61ECream#FAF5E9Fresh only#3C7D44

Keep the blue, the yellow and the plain speaking, and move them forward with space and restraint instead of chrome. The future Greggs should feel like one you already know.

Greggs has failed abroad once

  1. 2008Belgium shops close. A British menu and supply chain didn't travel.
  2. 2026A relaunch at Tenerife South airport with Lagardère
  3. 2050A local micro-bakery, a local menu, one global hero: the sausage roll

Don't export Britain. Fix the icon, let everything around it adapt.

How I used AI

Dozens of directions, one locked spec.

I wrote the brand spec down before generating anything, which kept 60+ images, films and sounds looking like one brand. I checked every output and fixed what the AI got wrong, like a broken logo. Strategy and decisions stayed with me.

  • Brand direction: Warm Functional FuturismKeep the blue and yellow. Go easy on chrome, and never overclaim.
